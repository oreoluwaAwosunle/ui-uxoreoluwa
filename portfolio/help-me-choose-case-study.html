Case study · AI concept

Help Me Choose

An AI-guided recommendation assistant built into my own phone resale business, turning budget uncertainty into a shortlist with real trade-offs, not another filter.

TypeFounder & product owner, personal exploration
FocusAI recommendation, personalization
ToolsHTML / JS prototyping, existing PhonePlugNG brand system

FIG 1 · The chat drawer mid-recommendation, phone matched to a stated budget and use case

01

Where it came from

PhonePlugNG already gets people from browsing to a WhatsApp chat, fast. That's the whole point of the catalog page I built. But the DMs kept circling back to the same question: what fits my budget? People don't shop in filter terms, they say “something good, not too expensive” or “content creation, around 150k.” A price slider can't hear that. An AI that can ask a follow-up and explain trade-offs, can.

02

The problem

120 items across 9 categories is a lot to scroll through without knowing what you're even looking for. People default to messaging me directly and asking me to just pick for them, which works, but doesn't scale past one conversation at a time, and every buyer starts from zero.

03

Proof this isn’t a one-off

83%
of shoppers who abandon a purchase say it's because they felt overwhelmed by too much choice online
55%
cite fear of picking wrong as a reason they abandon
10×
more likely to make a confident decision when shown fewer options instead of more

Source: UK consumer survey via iAdvize; fewer-options research via Bluebarry

04

How it solves it

A price filter can't parse “something good, not too expensive”
→
free-text input, with quick-reply chips for people who'd rather tap than type
One answer doesn't explain why
→
a shortlist of 2–3 phones, each with what it's good at and what you're trading off against the others
Recommending something whose price already changed
→
prices shown as last-confirmed, final number gets nailed down in the WhatsApp chat, same as today
A generic AI needs somewhere new to send people
→
hands straight into the same WhatsApp flow the business already runs on, not a separate checkout
See the working prototype

Grounded in real WhatsApp conversations, not tested with real users yet. Next step is running the actual free-text parsing against messages people really send, not the clean examples I wrote it against.
