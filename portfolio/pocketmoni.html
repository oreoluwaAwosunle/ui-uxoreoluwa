Case study, 003

PocketMoni: Community Savings & Referral

Supporting-role design work on two feature areas of eTranzact's mobile banking app, under lead product designer Seun Ogundipe.

TypeContributing designer, v1
FocusCommunity savings, referral
ToolsFigma
01

Role & context

PocketMoni is eTranzact's mobile banking app. I worked on two feature areas for v1 in a supporting capacity, under lead product designer Seun Ogundipe: a community savings flow (shared, goal-based contribution accounts) and the app's referral and feedback system.

This work was done on eTranzact's company design accounts, which I no longer have access to. What's shown here is what survived in my own archive, not a complete flow record, and it reflects v1 only.

02

Community savings flow

The flow lets a user set up a shared savings group and dedicate one of their existing accounts to it for the group's duration. The account-selection step was one of the details I worked on directly: framing it as a real commitment, with plain-language warning copy that the account won't be accessible while the community is active, rather than a throwaway settings toggle. Dedicating real money to a group needs that kind of clarity up front.

Once a community exists, members are added through a native-feeling share sheet routed to WhatsApp, Telegram, and Instagram, since group savings in Nigeria mostly gets organized through chat, not email invites.

03

Referral & feedback

The referral program pays out when an invited friend actually transfers over a set threshold, not just for signing up, which keeps the incentive tied to real usage rather than empty signups. I worked on the surrounding UI and copy for the reward mechanic and the invite-link screen.

The feedback prompt branches by sentiment: a negative response opens a plain text field asking what to improve, a positive one asks for an App Store rating. It's a small distinction, but it means the app isn't asking a frustrated user to go rate it publicly, and isn't wasting a happy user's moment asking them to explain themselves in a text box.
